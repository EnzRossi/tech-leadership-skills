**A firm launch date or startup/enterprise price is not supportable without scope.** “Global marketplace with AI” could describe very different delivery obligations. Company type alone is neither a cost nor a schedule multiplier.

Propose a **two-working-day discovery timebox** with one technical lead and an available product decision-maker. This is a planning allocation, not measured build effort. Define one initial market, buyer/seller journey, payments and operational boundaries, the purpose of AI, acceptance owner and a minimum launch outcome. Identify actual privacy, reliability, localization, approval and support constraints; inspect reusable platforms. End with accepted scope, dependencies and a representative slice to calibrate through agent review, fixes and human acceptance.

| Team | Conditional scenario after discovery |
|---|---|
| 1 engineer | One owner performs implementation, agent steering, integration and verification serially; feasible only if scope and operational support fit |
| 3 engineers | Can split buyer/seller experience, integration and verification after shared contracts are agreed; shared domain decisions remain serial |
| 8 engineers | Helpful only with enough independent accepted increments and reviewer capacity; onboarding and integration can erase gains |

No numeric build duration or price is justified yet. Price requires scoped human effort, supplied rates, agent/CI usage and operating costs. Startup and enterprise comparisons must use the same outcome, adjusting only concrete requirements and gates.

The next decision belongs to the product sponsor: what is the smallest launch journey and initial geography, what does the AI do, and who accepts and operates the result? Reforecast after a representative slice is accepted; do not commit the full team before that evidence.
