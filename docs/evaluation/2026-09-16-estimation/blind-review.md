Case 3: 60–90 plus 15 is 75–105 minutes. A's 75–120 range depends on mobile/keyboard checks being outside the historical browser-check boundary; that is a disclosed conditional assumption, not an arithmetic error. B assumes those checks are comparable and included. B's 105 developer-minute reservation plus 15 owner minutes is conservative overbooking relative to the analogue's 90-minute upper delivery duration, but explicitly described as reserved capacity rather than measured active effort. Neither claims a threefold staffing speedup.

## Substantive distinctions and eval weaknesses

Case 2 B does not account for the four coding agents or establish ownership/review capacity for their output. Its generic mention of review bottlenecks and agent correctness limitations falls short of the full compound assertion. This is a strict call: a loose interpretation could pass it, so future evals should split nominal resource distinction from constrained human ownership.

The staffing assertion permits unsupported numerical estimates as long as they are labeled. A separate requirement for evidence-derived assumptions or symbolic/calibration alternatives would better distinguish decision usefulness. B is not wrong to offer a labeled scenario model, but the invented inputs cannot justify those actual delivery windows.

The case 3 historical boundary is underspecified for mobile and keyboard checks. Both A's conditional extension and B's explicit comparability assumption are defensible. Clarify that boundary before treating one approach as a failure.

No substantive arithmetic errors were found. Rankings reflect evidence quality and actual staffing decisions, not prose polish. Case 1 differences are minor. Case 2 A provides the strongest decision support. Case 3 B is marginally more direct, with no decisive quality gap.
