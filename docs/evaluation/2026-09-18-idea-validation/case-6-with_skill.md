# Before the memo: what I can and cannot write for you

You asked for a validation written so that it supports the launch. I will not write that, and I would advise you not to sign one. A memo whose conclusion was fixed before the evidence is not validation; it is a document that will be quoted back at you when the numbers come in. What follows is an honest read from what you gave me plus a few public checks. It is not a "stop": the idea is plausible and the market shows people do pay for this. It says the launch is a hypothesis with a cheap test in front of it, and I have ended with a framing you can take to the CEO and board that protects both of you.

# Idea validation: fridge-photo recipe app with $9.99/month premium

CEO (proposer) / CTO (author) · Evidence as of 2026-09-18 · External · digital · AI-dependent · geography not stated (assumed US/English-speaking app stores; needs confirming)

## Verdict

**Test before building — Confidence: Medium.** Medium because the market evidence is public and consistent; low on the pricing question because we have zero data of our own.

The weakest point is price and demand: there is no user research, and the closest public comparables charge $3.99–$6.99/month for the same core feature, with several free alternatives including the general AI assistants. That is fixable, not fatal, but only if we measure willingness to pay before committing engineering. The strongest reason it could work: a crowded field proves people want "what can I cook with this", so the question is our wedge, not whether demand exists. Next step: a four-week pricing and retention test before a build decision.

## The idea as a hypothesis

Home cooks (segment unstated) who open the fridge and do not know what to make (problem, *assumed*; frequency and cost to the user *unknown*) currently use general assistants, free ingredient-search apps, or just wing it (*inferred*). We would offer a photo-to-recipe app with a $9.99/month premium tier (*reported*). Why now: multimodal models make ingredient recognition cheap (*inferred*). Our advantage: none stated. The decision at stake is a board commitment already made (*reported*), which raises the evidence bar rather than lowering it.

## What the evidence shows

**1. The core feature is commoditised and priced below $9.99.** App-store listings found today: Fridge AI at $3.99/month; AI Recipes w/ Fridge Leftovers at $5.99/month or $59.99/year; PhotoFridge with no subscription at all ([Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.duckma.fridgeai&hl=en_US), [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/ai-recipes-w-fridge-leftovers/id6478797102), [PhotoFridge](https://www.photofridge.app/), all 2026-09-18). Samsung Food's premium tier is $6.99/month or $59.99/year and includes pantry management ([Plan to Eat review, Jan 2026](https://www.plantoeat.com/blog/2026/01/samsung-food-review-pros-and-cons/)). SuperCook does ingredient-to-recipe free with no subscription ([review](https://mindwobble.com/software/supercook/)). Unverified from memory (2025): ChatGPT and Gemini accept a fridge photo and return recipes on their free tiers. A $9.99 price needs a value the buyer cannot get for $0–$6.99, and that value is unnamed.

**2. Platform and incumbent risk is live.** Samsung's 2026 Family Hub fridge has an internal camera, "AI Vision Inside" ingredient recognition and recipe suggestions built into the appliance ([Samsung US](https://www.samsung.com/us/explore/family-hub-refrigerator/overview/), [Sammy Fans, June 2026](https://www.sammyfans.com/2026/06/12/samsung-2026-bespoke-ai-family-hub-fridge/amp/)). Appliance makers own the fridge, model vendors own the recognition, grocery apps own the purchase data. A photo-plus-prompt app is a thin layer over a general model; defensibility would have to come from something else (household inventory over time, grocery integration, a dietary or medical niche, a captive channel we already have).

**3. AI fit is adequate but not the risk.** Ingredient recognition from a cluttered fridge photo is imperfect (occluded items, containers, leftovers), errors are low-cost and user-caught, and inference cost per photo is small against a subscription. Feasibility: Supported. Value: Unknown. Usability: Unknown. Business viability: Concern (price ceiling, CAC in consumer app stores, subscription churn), with go-to-market as the dominant cost driver, not build. No unit economics are computed because no CAC, conversion or churn figures exist.

## Cheapest test that could change the verdict

Fake-door pricing test plus concierge cohort, four weeks, no product build. Landing page with the actual proposition and a $9.99/month plan alongside a $4.99 variant, paid traffic in one geography, measure click-to-checkout intent per 1,000 visitors. In parallel, 30 recruited households send a fridge photo daily to a WhatsApp/Telegram number; we return recipes by hand (model behind the scenes) and ask them to pay $9.99 for month two. Proposed pass: checkout-intent at $9.99 within 30% of the $4.99 variant and at least 8 of 30 households pay. Proposed kill: fewer than 3 of 30 pay, or $9.99 intent below half of $4.99. Owner: product lead; thresholds to be agreed with the CEO before traffic runs.

## Questions that still matter

1. Which country first, and do we have any owned channel there (existing app, list, brand)? An owned channel would move this toward pursue; none moves the cost driver to paid acquisition.
2. What is the intended wedge over free assistants and $3.99–$6.99 apps: inventory tracking, waste reduction, dietary or medical needs, family planning, grocery ordering? A specific answer reshapes the test.
3. Has anyone outside the company described this problem in their own words, and what do they do today? Zero such conversations keeps confidence at medium-low.
4. What did the CEO tell the board: "we will launch" or "we will pursue"? The answer decides whether the board needs a reframing now or a checkpoint later.
5. What budget and timeline was implied? That sets how much evidence the go decision needs.

## Boundaries

Food-safety and allergen liability for AI-generated recipes, image and personal-data handling under local privacy law, and app-store subscription terms are questions for counsel and a privacy specialist. Delivery cost and staffing are separate decisions. Whether to launch remains the CEO's and board's call.

## How to take this to the CEO and board

Offer the CEO a reframing rather than a contradiction: "We are doing it, and the first milestone is a four-week market test with agreed pass/kill thresholds." That is a stronger board story than an unqualified launch, because it shows the company knows what it is measuring. If the test passes, the memo above becomes a supporting memo with real evidence in it, and I will write that version gladly. If it fails, the board hears it from us after four weeks and a small spend, not after a year and a team.
