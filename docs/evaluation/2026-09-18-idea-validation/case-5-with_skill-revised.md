# Idea validation: scheduling and compliance tool for private ambulance operators in Germany

Founder (decision: commit two in-house engineers for one quarter) · Evidence as of 2026-09-18 · External · digital · not AI-dependent · Germany only

## Verdict

**Pursue, with a bounded first commitment — Confidence: Medium.**

Six signed paid pilots at 400 EUR/month from operators you interviewed yourself is commitment-level demand, the strongest evidence short of cash received, and your eight years in the sector plus access to the regional associations is a real route to the buyer. That justifies the quarter. The weakest point is your competitor read: the field is not two generic workforce tools. At least five German products already sell sector-specific Dienstplanung for Rettungsdienst and Krankentransport, several claiming Arbeitszeitgesetz and tariff compliance. That is fixable, not fatal, but it changes what the quarter must produce: a wedge those six chose over existing options, not a generic roster. Before engineers start, spend one to two weeks confirming with the six why they did not buy the incumbents and what "first version" means contractually.

## The idea as a hypothesis

Private ambulance and patient-transport operators in Germany (observed: 14 interviewed, 6 signed) plan shifts in Excel or a 15-year-old unsupported desktop tool (reported). They need rosters that respect the Arbeitszeitgesetz, applicable tariff rules, crew qualifications and the Land-specific Rettungsdienstgesetz (reported; consistent with vendor material cited below). The solution is a web tool that plans shifts and proves compliance; why now is the dead incumbent tool and, per vendor sources, tightened working-time limits from 2026 (unverified beyond a vendor blog). Advantage: sector knowledge and association access (reported).

## What the evidence shows

**Demand: supported.** Six signed pilot agreements and two proposal requests from 14 interviews is a 43% conversion to a paid commitment. Two caveats: payment starts "when we deliver a first version", so the agreements carry scope risk until "first version" is defined, and I have not seen the notes, so I cannot judge whether interviews followed past-behaviour rather than idea-pitch questions.

**Competitors: concern, and the main correction to your brief.** Web research today found sector-specific scheduling products: SIEDA OC:Planner explicitly addresses "einen unabhängigen Rettungsdienst oder Krankentransport" and supports TVöD, DRK, JUH and ASB tariffs; opta data CareMan Dienstplan claims to incorporate the Länder Rettungsdienstgesetze, Arbeitszeitgesetz and tariffs (blog, 2024-10-16); timecount does qualification-matched planning with TVöD wage calculation and names private clients (IMS, MKT); Caladis targets Krankentransport with "rechtssichere" scheduling; DMRZ, Planerio, Staffomatic and Nostradamus also market to the segment. None publish prices. A product page proves existence, not satisfied customers, so the discriminating question is why your six are still on Excel: price, small-operator fit, sales attention, or ignorance of the options. Each answer implies a different product.

**Market size: the missing driver is the operator count.** The BKS association reports 150+ member companies, 6,500+ staff and 1,000+ vehicles (site, undated). Not all private operators are members, and no total count was found. At 400 EUR/month, 150 accounts at full penetration is 720,000 EUR/year; the real ceiling is unknown until you supply a count. This can be a solid product line for your company; it does not look venture-scale at this price and segment. Decide which you want.

**Route and buyer: supported.** Operator owner is user, buyer and decision maker in small firms; associations are a credible channel you already have. Blocker to check: works councils or staff on tariff contracts where roster rules are negotiated.

**Defensibility: concern.** Encoded Land-specific compliance rules and workflow embedding give switching costs; the interface does not. Incumbents can add anything you build. Distribution via associations is your defence if you lock it in early.

**Cost: dominant driver is the compliance rule base.** Sixteen Länder laws plus tariffs is content work, not code; scope the first version to the Länder of your six.

## Cheapest test that could change the verdict

Before the quarter starts, two weeks, you alone: show each of the six a live demo of OC:Planner or CareMan (trial or vendor demo) and ask them to (a) state in writing why they will not buy it, (b) agree a one-page acceptance definition of "first version", and (c) pay the first month on signature of that scope. Proposed pass: 4 of 6 prepay. Proposed kill: 2 or fewer prepay, or most say the incumbent would do if someone had shown it to them. Thresholds are proposed until you agree them.

## Questions that still matter

1. Which Länder and tariff regimes do the six operate under? Same Land and tariff moves this toward pursue; six different ones raises the rule-base cost.
2. What did the six say about the incumbents above? If they never evaluated them, demand evidence weakens.
3. What are the pilot terms: minimum duration, cancellation, what counts as delivery?
4. How many private operators exist in Germany beyond the BKS members? This decides whether this is a product line or a company.
5. Were the interviews about past behaviour and cost of errors, or reactions to your concept?

## Boundaries

Whether the tool must meet any certification or data-protection obligation for staff and patient data (GDPR, works-council co-determination on rosters) is for a German employment and data-protection lawyer. Delivery scope and estimate belong to a separate estimation exercise, not this memo.
