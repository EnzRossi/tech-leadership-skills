# Idea validation: Project-handoff app

External digital product · Small German design agencies

## Verdict

**Test before building — high confidence that this test is inconclusive.** Stop spending under the exhausted €150 cap, but do not kill the app on this evidence. The weakest point is that the experiment never gave the required eligible cohort a working opportunity to book. Those defects are fixable; demand remains unknown. Two eligible owners clicking “book” is the strongest positive signal, but it establishes interest, not paid-trial commitment.

## Decisive evidence

Your rule required at least three bookings from 30 eligible agency owners. You verified only four eligible owners among 400 visits, and the form was broken throughout. Zero completed bookings therefore cannot distinguish rejection of the offer from inability to book. Neither the general newsletter visits nor the two clicks substitute for the agreed denominator and outcome. This run also failed to establish a route that reaches enough verified buyers within the cap.

## One next step

Prepare one **new, bounded retest** before committing further spend: repair and verify the booking flow end to end, then propose direct recruitment of 30 verified owners of small German design agencies, keeping the same offer and paid-trial terms. Preserve the original three-of-30 rule; do not pool this broken run with the retest or keep buying traffic until three bookings appear.

Propose one team member as owner and a two-week window, with a fresh money and staff-time cap agreed before launch. These are planning proposals, not approved commitments. At the deadline: three or more completed paid-trial bookings from 30 valid eligible exposures supports proceeding to those trials; fewer than three with valid exposure and functioning booking stops that offer. Insufficient eligible exposure or another booking failure remains inconclusive: stop at the cap and park pending a viable test, without automatic extensions. Even a pass would not establish retention or broader demand.
