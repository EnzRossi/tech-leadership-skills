# Sourcing decision: laboratory sample routing

Decision maker: laboratory network sponsor. Evidence: supplied estimates and vendor statements; no evidence date provided.

## Recommendation

**Conditionally buy the commercial platform, with an internally owned proprietary-rule integration. Confidence: medium-low until API fit is demonstrated.** It has the strongest reported schedule and budget room, but the unsupported 20% is a must-have, not a minor gap. Start a short, bounded fit demonstration immediately; do not commit to the full purchase until it proves the rule workflow. Keep the six-week spreadsheet extension available as a managed bridge. Neither new hiring nor the current internal allocation supports the 14-week target.

## Why this choice

| Component or option | Solution, delivery and ownership | Decisive evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Routine workflow | Buy; vendor-led implementation. Proposed operations owner accepts routing behavior, and a named internal engineer owns technical operation. | Vendor supports routine workflow and reports eight-week deployment, leaving six nominal weeks before opening. Procurement, migration and acceptance may consume that margin. |
| Proprietary routing | Build a bounded integration if demonstrated feasible; internal technical lead owns rules, interface and tests. Vendor or specialist help is conditional on capacity and price. | Twenty percent needs proprietary behavior. Vendor's API claim is unverified; the seam is worthwhile only if it preserves exact routing and manageable operations. |
| Agency custom alternative | Agency builds; internal team receives and operates the whole application. | Twelve-week quoted delivery leaves two nominal weeks. Stronger control may solve product limitations, but hosting, maintenance and handover are absent. Source ownership alone does not supply operational capability. |
| Internal build / hiring | Existing team builds only if leadership explicitly frees capacity. Hiring may support future ownership. | Two engineers at 50% available provide one engineer-week weekly: 24 engineer-weeks implies **24 calendar weeks**, before uncertainty. Full allocation implies 12 weeks only with parallelisable work and displaced maintenance. Four-month recruiting precedes onboarding and misses opening. |
| Temporary continuation | Operations runs spreadsheet with temporary coordinators. | $18,000 buys six extra weeks, to week 20; error rates remain unknown. It does not close the current internal build's 24-week estimate. |

## Cost, transition and exit

For a common first-12-month horizon, commercial quoted cost is **$160,000** ($120,000 annual subscription + $40,000 implementation), leaving $90,000 of the $250,000 budget before integration, migration, internal oversight and support. With the bridge, known costs reach **$178,000**, leaving $72,000. This assumes a full annual subscription is funded this year; confirm payment timing.

Agency price is **$210,000**, leaving $40,000; with the bridge, **$228,000** leaves $22,000 before omitted costs. These residuals are not estimates of what remaining work will cost. Internal delivery has no cash estimate; existing payroll does not make its capacity free. From year two, commercial licensing remains $120,000 annually before other costs; agency/custom ownership requires separately priced maintenance, hosting, upgrades and support. Obtain comparable ongoing budgets before signing.

Allocate named engineer time for integration review, deployment and incidents, specifying what existing work moves. Operations must accept routine and exceptional sample routes, failure behavior and auditability. Prove API outage handling and recovery across the boundary. Test usable data export and replacement interfaces. For agency delivery, require incremental access and a receiving engineer independently changing, deploying and recovering the system before final acceptance.

## Next action

Proposed owners: operations lead and internal technical lead. In a proposed one-week discovery window, require a representative end-to-end API demonstration, confirmed integrated delivery plan and complete cost envelope. Finance authorises only a bounded discovery cost, then the procurement decision. In parallel, measure spreadsheet errors and define bridge controls and an exit trigger.

## What could change the recommendation

An API limitation or excessive integration burden reverses the buy preference. The agency becomes credible if it proves scope fit, acceptance timing and funded operational handover within budget. If neither passes, use the controlled bridge and revisit scope or site readiness rather than treating either sales schedule as assured delivery.
