# AI initiative decision: warranty claims

Decision maker: operations sponsor. Evidence: supplied scenario; no evidence date provided.

## Recommendation

**Investigate before purchasing. Confidence: high that production investment is premature; low on eventual AI value.** Use the available six weeks and one adjuster to establish the baseline, compare process improvement, and, conditional on permitted data access and no unapproved expenditure, evaluate a review-only assistant. The curated demo cannot justify a controlled live pilot. Even the vendor's savings claim leaves limited first-year headroom beyond quoted costs.

## Decisive evidence

From your figures, current effort is 2,400 × 12 / 60 = **480 hours/month**, valued at **$21,600/month**. A genuine 50% net reduction would release 240 hours, worth $10,800/month in capacity, not necessarily cash. Quoted first-year cost is $6,000 × 12 + $36,000 = **$108,000**, against $129,600 of claimed annual capacity value: only $21,600 remains before integration, oversight and errors. At that optimistic rate, implementation recovery takes 7.5 months after recurring fees, excluding other costs. First-year labor-value break-even requires a 41.7% net reduction, or five minutes per claim; review and correction must be inside that measure.

The vendor reports 94% agreement on 80 operations-selected claims, excluding missing documents. Approximately **720 of 2,400 live claims monthly** lack documents. Selection bias and potentially revised final decisions make this neither representative performance nor reliable ground truth. Do not extrapolate it into live error counts.

The strongest simpler alternative is a structured form plus deterministic completeness and policy checks. Its $12,000 cost is lower, but dealer adoption and avoided work are unknown. Test the intake approach before committing to implementation. Interpretation may warrant AI; arithmetic, eligibility constraints and required-document validation should remain exact, with an adjuster approving every decision.

## Good enough for the next stage

Agree an evaluation contract before testing:

- Compare existing handling, structured intake and human-plus-assistant on equivalent claims. Measure total handling, follow-up, inspection, review and correction time, rework, adoption and cost per resolved claim.
- Reserve untouched cases before tuning; include natural missing-document prevalence, ambiguous claims and subsequent inspection changes. Keep separate stress cases for costly mistakes. Prevent related claims or later inspection information leaking into decision-time inputs.
- Have a qualified independent claims reviewer adjudicate disputed labels against policy and evidence available at each decision stage. One adjuster can discover issues but cannot provide independent adjudication alone.
- Report incorrect approvals, incorrect denials, unsupported statements, escalation and reviewer detection by case type, with denominators and uncertainty. Set tolerances with operations based on incumbent errors and relationship consequences; 94% agreement is not an acceptance threshold. Each incremental incorrect approval consumes roughly $900; denial costs remain unquantified.

## Next commitment

Proposed owner: operations manager, with the available adjuster. Within six weeks, produce a comparison and whole-workflow business case. Keep outputs in shadow mode, with current handling as fallback; verify data permissions before vendor access. Additional reviewer time or vendor charges require a separately approved cap.

Advance only if representative held-out results meet agreed error tolerances, reviewers demonstrably catch consequential mistakes, and measured net value covers complete costs. Otherwise stop or redesign around intake improvement. A small experiment can expose failures, not certify rare-error safety.

## What could change the recommendation

Measured reductions in expensive errors or follow-up could strengthen the AI case beyond labor value. Strong form adoption could remove much of the need. Obtain both through the comparison; neither benefit is established today.
