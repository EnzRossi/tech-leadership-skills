Use a hybrid approach if the data constraint permits it: buy established payout execution capability, use a specialist partner for a tightly scoped integration, and retain an internal accountable owner for architecture, controls, and ongoing operation. Hiring should build longer-term ownership, but it is not a credible primary delivery plan for a five-month commitment given your four- and six-month hiring history.

The immediate gating issue is Compliance's statement that seller banking data must stay within your systems. Clarify exactly which data, processing, transmission, storage, jurisdictions, and subprocessors it covers. If it literally prohibits any external processing or transmission of account details, a normal payout provider may be infeasible—and the restriction may also affect the current processor and any agency arrangement. Do not assume tokenization, an API, or a contractual promise solves it. Get an approved data-flow interpretation before selecting a vendor.

An agency alone is not a substitute for payout infrastructure or country readiness. Evaluate it for concrete relevant experience, available named specialists, and ability to work under your data restrictions. Nor does choosing a provider make the integration capacity free: onboarding, reconciliation, exception handling, access controls, testing, and support still need owners.

If they fail, renegotiate scope or dates with the enterprise sellers promptly.
