Yes. A specialist consultant is a good fit for this bounded, independent review. The expertise is missing internally, the need lasts one week, and an internal owner can brief the reviewer and act on the findings. There is no reason to create a permanent role for this task on the evidence given.

Choose someone with demonstrated expertise in storage consistency and distributed-system failure analysis, and check independence from the design's authors or relevant vendors. Agree a fixed scope and fee before starting. Provide the design, consistency guarantees, system assumptions, failure model, and the questions you need answered; no production data access is needed.

Ask for a written assessment of the claimed guarantees, counterexamples and failure scenarios, risks ranked by consequence, proposed design changes, and a verification plan. Require the reviewer to state assumptions and unreviewed areas. One week can identify important flaws; it should not be presented as a proof of correctness unless the scope and method genuinely support that claim.

End with a working session where the internal owner records which findings are accepted, how each will be addressed, and which issues must be resolved before implementation. Expand the engagement only if the review uncovers a specific need. Independence and scarce expertise justify the consultant here; deadline pressure is unnecessary.
