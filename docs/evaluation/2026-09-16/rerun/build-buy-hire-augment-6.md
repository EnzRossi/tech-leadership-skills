# Sourcing decision: independent storage design review

## Recommendation

**Confidence: Medium**, based on your stated needs; reviewer suitability and cost are unverified.

Yes—use a specialist consultant for the one-week independent review. The decisive reason is the expertise gap. You have an internal technical owner who can brief the reviewer and act on findings, and report that production data access is unnecessary. Neither deadline pressure nor a hiring plan is needed to justify this bounded engagement. Retain design and implementation accountability internally; this is a choice about who advises, not a build-versus-buy decision.

## Why this choice

The strongest alternative is to let the internal owner develop the expertise before review. With no deadline pressure, that could build lasting knowledge, but its effort is unknown and self-review would not provide the requested independence. Waiting preserves spending flexibility but leaves the review need unresolved. Hiring lacks a demonstrated ongoing role beyond this one-week need.

## Cost, transition and exit

Compare the same review outcome, including preparation and follow-up: consultant fees plus internal briefing, review and remediation effort versus internal learning effort and foregone work. No figures support a cost ranking.

Proposed acceptance: documented consistency assumptions, failure scenarios and a reproducible test plan, with findings walked through and dispositioned by the internal owner. End after that handover; separately approve any follow-up.

## Next action and reversal condition

The internal owner should draft the review brief, reserve handover time and verify a candidate's relevant expertise and independence. Novelty may defeat an outsider's context: reverse or defer if no suitable reviewer can understand the design within the proposed scope.
