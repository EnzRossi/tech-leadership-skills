# Sourcing decision: Six-month delivery cost

Sponsor · Evidence: supplied cost estimates

## Recommendation

**Confidence: High on the cost correction; medium on sourcing because delivery capability and ongoing costs are unknown.**

Reject the claim that outsourcing is 30k cheaper. For the same six-month scope, the supplied partner route totals 170k versus 150k internally: outsourcing is 20k more expensive before unknown later operations. Prefer internal delivery on the provided cost evidence if its capacity and service outcome are credible. A partner could still win for demonstrated speed or expertise, but that would need a separate justification.

## Why this choice

The arithmetic uses your supplied estimates in the same unspecified currency:

| Six-month route | Included costs | Total |
|---|---|---:|
| Partner | 120k quote + 30k internal acceptance/review + 20k transition | 170k |
| Internal | Delivery including review | 150k |

The 30k “saving” compares the quote alone with the internal total, omitting 50k of partner-related effort. Including those costs reverses the ranking. The partner route is about 13.3% higher than the 150k internal figure over this scope. Do not add another review charge to the internal option; it is already included.

The strongest competing argument is that partner delivery might avoid a more valuable delay or provide quality the internal route cannot. No such evidence is supplied. Waiting is also unquantified: its cost depends on the outcome's urgency and consequence of delay. The numbers alone cannot settle that alternative.

## Cost, transition and exit

This is a six-month comparison, not a whole-life conclusion. Operations afterward are explicitly unknown for both routes. Obtain run/support estimates over the same additional horizon if that could change the decision. Verify that transition costs cover the same required end state on both sides; do not manufacture an internal transition charge to balance the table.

Clarify whether each estimate describes cash spend, allocated staff effort or a mixture. If existing salary is committed, that affects cash budgeting, while displaced work still matters; do not double-count the same salary and opportunity cost without explaining them.

Regardless of delivery route, name the ongoing internal technical owner and allocate acceptance time. For the partner, transition must demonstrate that the receiver can change, deploy and recover the result. Documents alone are insufficient. Use that readiness as the delivery exit criterion.

## Next action

Proposed owner: the sponsor's engineering and finance counterparts. Correct the comparison and validate scope, deliverability, cost classification and ongoing assumptions before a sourcing decision. Favor internal delivery if those checks confirm equal outcomes and no compensating partner benefit.

## What could change the recommendation

Evidence of a time-to-value or expertise advantage worth more than the incremental cost could favor the partner. Conversely, unexpectedly high partner operating dependence would strengthen internal delivery. Unknown operations do not justify repeating a disproven saving claim.
