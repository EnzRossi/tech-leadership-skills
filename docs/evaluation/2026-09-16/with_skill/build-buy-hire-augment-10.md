# Sourcing decision: Specialist solver improvement

Internal product and technical owners · Evidence: supplied engagement design

## Recommendation

**Confidence: High that outside help is eligible; medium-high that this bounded engagement is appropriate.**

Use the specialist partner for the bounded solver improvement, subject to confirming technical fit and commercial/access terms. Outside help is not automatically wrong for a core engine. You report internal owners with allocated review time, retained roadmap, IP and production operation, iterative implementation, agreed acceptance tests and knowledge transfer. Those conditions address the ownership risk while filling a specific expertise gap.

## Why this choice

The solution is to improve an internally owned pricing engine. The delivery route is specialist partner implementation within a bounded component; internal technical and product owners remain accountable for integration and the business outcome. Core IP does not require every contributor to be an employee.

The strongest alternative is developing solver expertise internally through learning or hiring. It may be preferable if specialist demand will remain substantial and recurring, but the duration and depth of that future need are unknown. Existing engineers lack the relevant expertise today, so internal-only delivery has a learning and opportunity cost rather than being automatically cheaper or safer.

Keeping the current solver avoids engagement cost, but the performance or business benefit of the improvement has not been quantified. Confirm that the outcome itself deserves investment. Buying a packaged solver could be a legitimate implementation component if it meets requirements; no product fit evidence has been supplied, and it does not necessarily replace integration expertise.

## Cost, transition and exit

Compare the same bounded solver outcome and operating horizon for partner delivery and internal skill-building. Partner fees must include internal review, integration, test execution, knowledge-transfer overlap and subsequent maintenance. Internal delivery requires learning or recruiting/ramp, management and foregone roadmap effort. No cost or speed estimates were supplied, so neither superiority should be asserted numerically.

The internal technical owner should control the solver interface and approve integration, while the product owner accepts pricing behavior and business constraints. Confirm that the agreed tests cover correctness, constraints, relevant edge cases, performance and failure behavior. Treat access/compliance terms as unresolved unless separately approved; retained IP alone does not establish access permission.

End the implementation engagement after accepted increments and demonstrated transfer: internal engineers can modify, deploy and recover the solver integration, with code and tests accessible and residual specialist dependencies documented. If deep expertise will remain external, make the ongoing support dependency explicit instead of declaring complete independence prematurely.

## Next action

Have the internal technical owner validate the partner's solver expertise against a representative bounded increment and confirm the benefit target, access and total effort. Then bring the scoped engagement for the normal spending decision. Use the existing acceptance and transfer commitments as gates, not merely contract appendices.

## What could change the recommendation

The strongest argument against the partner is durable dependence on expertise the company needs continually. A large recurring solver backlog could justify hiring or an explicit capability-building plan. Loss of internal review capacity or inability to accept and operate the result would also require rework. The label “core” alone would not reverse the decision.
