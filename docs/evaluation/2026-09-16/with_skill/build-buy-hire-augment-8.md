# Sourcing decision: Migration and durable platform role

Engineering manager · Evidence: your staffing and backlog description

## Recommendation

**Confidence: High on rejecting a mandatory contract rule; medium-high on hiring as the preferred route.**

Use the accepted candidate for the durable platform role, assuming their capabilities fit the migration and the reported start is dependable. You do not need to contract merely because the first assignment lasts eight weeks. The funded two-year backlog and available manager establish an ongoing role, while both candidate and agency can start next week. There is no supplied agency timing advantage.

## Why this choice

The solution remains delivery of the migration and platform backlog; the sourcing choice is who develops and sustains that capability. Hiring creates continuing ownership and reduces handover between the initial migration and subsequent work. The available manager supplies a necessary management condition that is often absent from hiring proposals.

The strongest competing option is an agency engineer for the migration. That may win if the agency has demonstrably superior migration expertise or the candidate cannot become productive in time. Starting on the same date does not prove equal time to usable capacity: compare actual experience, system familiarity, access and ramp. Those details are unknown, so the recommendation is conditional on fit rather than assumed productivity.

Waiting or reallocating existing staff has no demonstrated advantage here. It could be appropriate if the migration need disappears or a stronger internal capacity option exists, but neither is supplied. A hire plus an agency should not be added by default; it requires a separate bounded gap.

## Cost, transition and exit

Compare delivery and operation across the funded two-year demand, including the first eight weeks. For hiring, include compensation, benefits, ramp and management; separate already-incurred recruiting costs from future avoidable spend. For the agency, include fees, onboarding, review, handover and whoever performs the remaining backlog. No rates are supplied, so cost superiority is unproven.

The platform manager is the proposed accountable owner. Give the incoming engineer clear migration acceptance and ongoing priorities, access and pairing support. Migration acceptance should include validated data/system behavior, deployment and recovery readiness. After acceptance, move into the funded backlog with responsibility explicit. Review the staffing decision if the durable work or candidate fit materially changes, rather than impose an arbitrary contractor end date.

## Next action

Have the manager validate candidate fit and readiness against the migration's critical work and confirm the two-year allocation. If that check passes, proceed with the hiring route through the organization's normal employment process. Keep the agency option available only for a demonstrated capacity or expertise shortfall.

## What could change the recommendation

The strongest reason to use the agency is a proven skill or ramp advantage that protects a consequential migration deadline. Evidence that the funded backlog will not materialize would also weaken the durable role. The eight-week project duration by itself changes neither the long-term need nor the reported availability.
