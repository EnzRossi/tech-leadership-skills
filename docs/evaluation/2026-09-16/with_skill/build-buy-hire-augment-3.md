# Sourcing decision: Pricing and risk engine

CTO and executive sponsor · Evidence: your description and reported firm offer

## Recommendation

**Confidence: Medium-high on durable internal ownership; medium on the delivery mix.**

Do not accept the fixed-price rebuild as the complete answer. Establish a durable internal product and technical team for the engine, using hiring or explicit reassignment, and assess the known firm for bounded delivery support. You report that this engine drives customer choice and needs weekly evolution after a 12–18-month rebuild, while its sole expert has no time. A delivery contract does not solve that ownership and knowledge bottleneck.

## Why this choice

Retain and evolve an internally owned capability. Buying a replacement could be sensible for utility components but no product has been shown to preserve your differentiating behavior. Outsourcing the entire future capability would require evidence of sustainable change and knowledge ownership that the attractive rebuild offer does not supply.

The firm has meaningful advantages: it knows your codebase, has an existing relationship and reportedly meets compliance requirements. Outside work is therefore eligible, not automatically excluded because the engine is core. It could accelerate migration or provide specialist implementation while your team owns business rules, architecture, acceptance and production operation.

The strongest competing option is partner-led iterative delivery with a staffed internal receiving team. It may beat recruiting on time to usable capacity, but hiring speed and existing internal availability are unknown. Fixed price may fit genuinely bounded increments; it does not remove uncertainty across a long rebuild and ongoing weekly changes. Do not confuse a modern stack with a demonstrated need for wholesale replacement. Continuing with the time-constrained CTO preserves a known concentration of knowledge.

## Cost, transition and exit

Compare both routes across the rebuild plus the same explicitly chosen operating horizon afterward. The reported price is attractive but no figures are supplied. Include internal rule clarification and acceptance, data migration, parallel operation, production support, knowledge transfer and later changes alongside fees. Compare that with hiring, ramp, compensation, management effort and delay; avoid assuming either route is cheaper.

Proposed long-term owners are an internal engine technical lead and a product/risk decision owner, with actual allocations to be confirmed. Free CTO time for structured knowledge extraction and review; an unavailable founder cannot serve as the only acceptance mechanism. Deliver incrementally with behavioral tests and controlled cutover. Acceptance should include the receiving team independently modifying a rule, deploying and recovering the engine. Review partner scope at accepted capability boundaries and preserve code, tests and operational access throughout.

## Next action

Proposed owner: CTO with the executive sponsor. Produce a staffing and knowledge-transfer plan plus one bounded migration slice with acceptance evidence before deciding on the offer. Compare internal delivery and an iterative partner-supported route against that slice. Defer the full commitment if nobody can own and accept the output.

## What could change the recommendation

The strongest argument for the firm is its existing knowledge and faster credible delivery. Verified staffed ownership and a workable iterative agreement could make it the preferred implementation route. Conversely, an available internal team and no time advantage would favor internal delivery. Neither outcome removes the durable ownership requirement.
