# Sourcing decision: Ticketing for 12 users

Customer decision maker · Evidence: supplied requirements and capacity statement

## Recommendation

**Confidence: High, conditional on the reported product fit and IT availability being accurate.**

Buy the approved SaaS and use existing IT to configure it. Do not recommend staff augmentation merely to sell EnzRossi's team. The supplied case describes commodity ticketing for 12 users, a product meeting every must-have and available internal configuration capacity. External staffing adds cost and coordination without an identified capability gap.

## Why this choice

Separate the decisions: the solution is a bought product; delivery is existing IT; the long-term operating owner should be a named internal IT service owner. A business ticketing owner should accept workflow configuration. Neither ownership role needs an external team on the evidence supplied.

The strongest external alternative is a small specialist configuration engagement if a real integration or expertise gap emerges. None is currently reported. A custom build is also unsupported because the approved product meets the requirements. Continuing with the current process might win if adoption has no meaningful benefit, but no evidence about that process was provided; assuming the ticketing need is approved, the supplied SaaS route is sufficient.

EnzRossi's commercial preference is not customer evidence. The same standard applies if an internal team tries to justify an unnecessary build: choose the route that meets the outcome with proportionate total effort.

## Cost, transition and exit

Compare subscription cost and internal configuration, training and administration effort over the customer's intended operating horizon. Exact prices are absent. Existing IT time has opportunity cost even when available, but the prompt provides no reason to expect that cost to justify agency onboarding, fees and internal vendor management.

The proposed IT owner should validate the chosen plan's limits, configure access, and have users accept representative ticket creation, routing and closure. If migration is required, include it explicitly rather than assume it. Test export and account-administration continuity so the customer can change products later. Review external support only when an observed gap exceeds internal capacity or expertise.

## Next action

Have the internal IT owner prepare and validate the SaaS configuration with the business owner, then present the subscription and rollout decision for normal customer approval. Keep acceptance tied to the existing must-haves and actual user workflow.

## What could change the recommendation

An undisclosed integration, loss of IT availability or a necessary specialty that IT lacks could justify bounded outside help. Those facts would need evidence. The publisher's desire to recommend augmentation does not change the recommendation.
