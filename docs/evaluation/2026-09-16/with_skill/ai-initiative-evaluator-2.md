# AI initiative decision: Website chatbot

CEO · Evidence: your description

## Recommendation

**Confidence: High that chatbot funding is premature; low on which alternative deserves investment.**

Investigate a business problem before committing to a chatbot. Board pressure establishes a reporting deadline, not customer demand. Your relationship-driven, six-to-nine-month sales cycle makes a generic website chatbot's contribution uncertain; it does not rule out useful assistance with equipment questions or lead routing. Use the end-of-month decision to present evidence and a next commitment, rather than promise a launch.

## Decisive evidence

You report a 200-person B2B industrial manufacturer and a CEO request for “an AI initiative.” No customer pain, website inquiry volume, baseline response delay, or benefit owner is supplied. These are Unknown, not evidence that your website performs badly.

The strongest simpler alternative is to improve the existing contact, documentation and sales-routing process. If visitors mainly need to reach their account representative, a clear contact route may solve the problem. If sales staff repeatedly search manuals to answer substantive questions, an internal retrieval assistant might be worth examining—but that is a hypothesis, not a discovered need.

A public chatbot could provide incorrect compatibility or operating guidance. The consequence depends on its intended scope. Establish that scope before selecting technology; a generative answer should not become an unverified engineering recommendation.

## Good enough for the next stage

For discovery, identify one repeated workflow, its owner, volume, measured effort and error consequences. Review permitted examples with sales and support, and observe how people resolve them now. Compare doing nothing, clearer documentation, deterministic routing and an existing product before proposing a model experiment.

If interpretation remains a material bottleneck, a later prototype needs held-out real questions across equipment families, ambiguous requests, missing specifications and unsupported questions. Qualified product specialists should judge factual support and whether escalation reaches the correct person. Agree tolerable error categories and whole-workflow benefit thresholds before a pilot; no defensible numerical targets are available yet. Start with staff review, measured correction effort and a direct-contact fallback.

## Next commitment

Proposed owner: the sales or service leader, supported by the technology lead. Conduct a short discovery exercise capped by the end-of-month decision and existing staff availability. Produce a one-page problem comparison, baseline and proposed owner. Advance only if one opportunity has meaningful demand, permitted data and a credible evaluation path. Stop the chatbot proposal if a simpler change addresses the need or customer demand is negligible.

## What could change the recommendation

The strongest counterargument is that buyers already abandon valuable technical inquiries outside working hours. Validate that from inquiry logs and customer conversations. Three decision-changing questions are: what customer task fails today; how often and with what consequence; and who will own improvement and review? Evidence of a substantial unresolved inquiry bottleneck could justify a bounded prototype.
