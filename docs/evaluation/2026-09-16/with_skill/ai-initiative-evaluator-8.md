# AI initiative decision: Drafting pilot expansion

Support sponsor · Evidence: supplied timing measurements

## Recommendation

**Confidence: High on the arithmetic; medium on the broader product decision because quality evidence is absent.**

Do not expand or claim 4,000 minutes of weekly net savings. End-to-end handling increased from seven to eight minutes per ticket. On the supplied workload, the pilot consumes an additional 1,000 minutes a week. Return to the baseline workflow or contain the pilot while investigating whether a specific correctable failure explains the review burden.

## Decisive evidence

Using your supplied timings and 1,000 tickets per week:

- Before: 6 minutes drafting + 1 minute review = 7 minutes per ticket, or 7,000 minutes weekly.
- Pilot: 2 minutes drafting + 6 minutes review/correction = 8 minutes per ticket, or 8,000 minutes weekly.
- Drafting improves by 4,000 minutes weekly, while review adds 5,000. Net change is 1,000 additional minutes, approximately 16 hours 40 minutes, or 14.3% more handling effort than baseline.

These are effort measures, not demonstrated cash flows or headcount effects. No additional measured benefit offsets the increase. The strongest simpler option is the existing drafting process; reusable templates or better source material may be worth examining if a known repetitive bottleneck emerges.

## Good enough for the next stage

The current evidence does not justify broad deployment. If a narrow remediation is proposed, compare human-only and human-plus-tool on equivalent, representative tickets, including difficult cases. Keep a held-out set untouched by prompt tuning. Qualified support reviewers should assess answer correctness and resolution quality with a common rubric; measure review and correction time, reopens, escalation and total cost per successfully resolved ticket.

Proposed acceptance principle: expansion must recover net end-to-end capacity without unacceptable quality loss, or demonstrate a separately valued quality benefit large enough for the sponsor to accept the extra effort. Exact trade-offs require sponsor agreement. A drafting-only target is insufficient. Any live test should remain review-only with an available baseline fallback and bounded reviewer capacity.

## Next commitment

Proposed owner: the support operations lead. Correct the sponsor's savings statement and review a bounded sample of correction-heavy tickets to identify whether a feasible remedy exists. Do not launch another broad experiment automatically. Advance only if a specific repair merits a controlled comparison; stop the AI workflow if correction burden persists or no compensating benefit is demonstrated.

## What could change the recommendation

The strongest counterargument is that longer review produces better resolutions, or reviewers are temporarily learning. Neither is measured here. Reliable evidence of materially better outcomes, or a subsequent representative comparison showing sustained net effort reduction after learning, could justify reconsideration. The current claim remains false even if those hypotheses later prove true.
