# Project health decision: contractual delivery

Board · Evidence as of the supplied account; specific dates not supplied

## Recommendation

**Health: Red for the existing delivery plan · Confidence: Medium overall.** Report that three milestones have been missed, the customer commitment remains unchanged, and no evidenced recovery path supports the green claim. The plan has already breached milestones. Do not say the contractual customer date has already been missed or is certainly impossible: its date and remaining work are unknown. Require a substantiated recovery decision this week and report the commitment as exposed until that evidence exists.

## Findings that change the decision

| Evidence and source | Implication for the commitment | Confidence / competing explanation |
|---|---|---|
| User reports the last three milestones missed. | Repeated breaches of the existing plan contradict an unqualified on-track claim. | Strong reported evidence of missed milestones; they could contain recoverable float, so final-date failure is not yet established. |
| Original customer date remains contractual; no scope or date change agreed. | Internal optimism or a renamed plan cannot change the external commitment. | The exact target, milestone relationships and recovery capacity are unknown. |
| Steering slide copies the PM's green status. | It adds no independent support to the forecast. | A copied report may reflect normal reporting practice, not deception; status evidence is still missing. |

A candidate mechanism is that delayed prerequisite work compresses integration, acceptance or release time while the external deadline stays fixed. A competing explanation is that milestones were intermediate and sufficient slack or completed recovery work preserves the customer date. The discriminating check is an evidence-backed remaining dependency plan, including completed recovery actions and the time needed for customer acceptance. Do not infer the cause is poor engineering, morale or dishonesty from status disagreement alone.

## Suggested board wording

“Delivery status is Red against the existing plan: the last three milestones were missed. The contractual customer date and agreed scope remain unchanged. The green steering status repeats the PM's assessment and is not supported by a demonstrated recovery path in the material available. We cannot yet quantify final-date exposure. This week management will validate remaining acceptance and release work and decide whether a credible recovery is possible or whether a customer commitment change must be pursued.”

## This week

| Action or leadership decision | Proposed owner / needed by | Evidence or outcome produced |
|---|---|---|
| Reconcile original/current commitments, missed milestone dates and accepted work. | Delivery lead and product owner / within two working days | Factual baseline distinguishing actual breaches from future exposure. |
| Demonstrate a remaining dependency and acceptance plan with owners, durations and recovery evidence. | Engineering and delivery leads / within three working days | Credible route to the customer date or a specific feasibility gap. |

| Correct the steering status and attach the underlying evidence. | Sponsor and PM / next board update | Consistent reporting of actual milestones, current exposure and decision dates. |

Do not add people or replace leadership merely to make the status look responsive. Additional staff require separable work, available review capacity and a contribution before the bottleneck binds. Cutting acceptance requirements to preserve a date would change the commitment and requires an explicit decision.

## What could change the recommendation

A credible recovery plan showing available slack, independently demonstrated progress and sufficient acceptance time could support Amber or Green for the future customer date. It would not erase historical milestone misses. The most consequential missing facts are the contractual date and scope, the remaining dependency path, and completed recovery evidence. A meeting scheduled to develop a plan is not that evidence.
